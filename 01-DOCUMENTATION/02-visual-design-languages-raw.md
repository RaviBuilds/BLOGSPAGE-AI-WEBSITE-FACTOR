**PHASE 2 &mdash; FIVE VISUAL DESIGN LANGUAGES**

**2.0 Purpose**

Phase 2 defines the **five visual grammars** that sit above the global
Foundation.

The Foundation provides:

- technical consistency

- accessibility

- responsive behavior

- structural primitives

- quality constraints

The Design Language provides:

- visual personality

- composition philosophy

- typography personality

- spacing rhythm

- image behavior

- interaction character

- visual tension

- creative range

- section behavior

- storytelling style

The Business Research provides:

- business identity

- customer psychology

- content

- imagery

- trust signals

- conversion requirements

The AI then combines all three.

GLOBAL FOUNDATION

&darr;

VISUAL DESIGN LANGUAGE

&darr;

BUSINESS CONTEXT

&darr;

UNIQUE WEBSITE

The goal is **not** to make five templates.

The goal is to make five different ways of designing.

**2.1 The Core Principle**

**Composition over Configuration**

A component should not dictate the final appearance.

The AI should be able to take the same underlying primitive and create
different compositions.

For example:

Image + Heading + CTA

could become:

Editorial:

offset editorial composition

or:

Swiss:

structured grid composition

or:

Bold:

oversized typographic composition

or:

Soft:

organic layered composition

or:

Architectural:

monumental full-bleed composition

The research explicitly recommends this composition-driven model,
including reusable primitives that accept arbitrary content rather than
rigid section-specific props.

**2.2 The Five Languages**

| **ID** | **Visual Language** | **Core Concept** | **Primary Emotional Driver** |
|:--:|----|----|----|

**DL-01 &mdash; EDITORIAL LUXURY**

**2.4.1 Philosophy**

**Design the website like a premium editorial publication.**

The website should feel curated, intentional and art-directed.

The research associates this language with magazine-style asymmetry,
serif typography, expansive whitespace and offset imagery, particularly
for high-end salons, cosmetic dentistry and fine dining.

**Emotional response**

The user should think:

&ldquo;This feels sophisticated.&rdquo;

&ldquo;These people care about details.&rdquo;

&ldquo;This feels expensive.&rdquo;

**2.4.2 Visual DNA**

Elegant

Editorial

Asymmetric

Refined

Quiet

Layered

Intentional

Human

Curated

**2.4.3 Composition Philosophy**

**Break the grid without destroying the grid.**

Preferred:

- offset elements

- asymmetrical alignment

- image/text overlap

- large visual anchors

- deliberate empty space

- unexpected proportions

- unusual image crops

- occasional edge bleed

Avoid:

- rigid symmetrical layouts throughout

- equal-sized repeated cards

- predictable section blocks

**2.4.4 Typography DNA**

Primary:

**high-contrast serif**

Secondary:

**neutral modern sans**

Typography should create:

- editorial contrast

- dramatic scale

- elegant line breaks

- sophisticated hierarchy

Display type can itself become a compositional element.

**2.4.5 Spacing Rhythm**

quiet

&rarr; expansive

&rarr; intimate

&rarr; dramatic

&rarr; expansive

Whitespace should feel **luxurious**, not empty.

**2.4.6 Image DNA**

Best suited to:

- portraits

- architecture

- environmental photography

- lifestyle imagery

- premium service photography

Cropping can be expressive.

**Make precision itself look premium.**

This language communicates competence through structure.

The research describes it as utilitarian, highly legible, grid-oriented
and predictable, making it especially suitable for medical, legal and
professional services.

**The grid is visible.**

Use:

- strong alignment

- mathematical proportions

- clear modules

- horizontal lines

- structured columns

- repeated alignment anchors

**2.9 The Crucial Difference Between the Five**

The shortest way to remember them:

EDITORIAL

Break the grid.

SWISS

Control the grid.

BOLD

Compress and energize the grid.

SOFT

Flow through the grid.

ARCHITECTURAL

Construct the grid.

This is much more useful than saying:

Editorial = serif

Swiss = sans

Bold = neon

Soft = beige

Architectural = black

because **composition**, rather than color, is what gives the system
real distinction.

**2.10 Primary + Secondary Influence**

A website may use:

Primary Language

\+

Secondary Influence

Example:

\+

Architectural

Soft Premium

\+

Editorial

Bold Energetic

\+

Architectural

The secondary influence should modify the composition without destroying
the primary language.

Example:

70% Editorial

20% Architectural

10% Business expression

This gives us substantially more creative combinations.

**2.11 Compatibility Rules**

**Strong combinations**

Editorial + Architectural

Editorial + Soft

Swiss + Soft

Swiss + Architectural

Bold + Architectural

Soft + Editorial

**Caution**

Bold + Swiss

Bold + Soft

Architectural + Soft

**Hard restriction**

A secondary influence must never contradict the business psychology.

For example, the research explicitly warns that aggressive gym
aesthetics should not be transferred into dental/medical environments
where they can increase anxiety rather than reassurance.

**2.14 Asset-Aware Design**

The AI must evaluate:

Photography quality

Photography quantity

Video availability

Logo quality

Brand consistency

People photography

Facility photography

Before/after availability

before committing to a design direction.

For example:

Strong photography + video

&rarr; Architectural / Bold highly viable

Strong portraits + refined photography

&rarr; Editorial highly viable

Weak photography

&rarr; Swiss highly viable

Warm people photography

&rarr; Soft highly viable

This prevents the system from choosing a visually beautiful language
that the business does not have the assets to support.

**2.19 Creativity Hierarchy**

The AI must always prioritize:

1\. Business truth

2\. Customer needs

3\. Conversion

4\. Usability

5\. Accessibility

6\. Brand identity

7\. Design language

8\. Creative expression

But once levels 1&ndash;7 are satisfied:

**The AI should actively search for the most distinctive tasteful
composition available.**

This is the rule that protects your emphasis on creativity.

**2.20 Novelty Requirement**

Before finalizing a site, the AI should ask:

**&ldquo;Does this look like a generic website I've generated
before?&rdquo;**

It should compare its own composition choices against previous projects
where possible.

Check:

Hero composition

Section sequence

Grid structure

Typography treatment

Image placement

CTA style

Card strategy

Gallery strategy

Rhythm

If too many are repeated:

**regenerate the composition, not merely the colors.**

This is one of the strongest safeguards we can build into the eventual
AI Website Factory.

**2.21 Universal Anti-Template Rules**

Regardless of language:

DO NOT:

- automatically use a centered hero

- automatically use two hero buttons

- automatically create a 3-card service grid

- round every element

- use generic gradients

- create identical bento cards

- alternate image-left / text-right endlessly

- use excessive shadows

- invent testimonials

- invent statistics

- use meaningless animation

- compress desktop into mobile

The research explicitly identifies these as important AI anti-patterns.
